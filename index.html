Engineered enzyme therapy · Glioblastoma

Cutting off glioblastoma's antioxidant fuel supply, from the bloodstream.

Enzara Therapeutics is developing Cyst(e)inase, an engineered human enzyme that degrades circulating cysteine and cystine. Glioblastoma cells rely on systemic supply to protect themselves from ferroptosis. The enzyme works in the blood, so it does not need to cross the blood–brain barrier.

Why Enzara

A different route to an old problem

The need

Glioblastoma is the most common malignant primary brain tumor in adults. Even with surgery, radiation and temozolomide, median survival is around 15 months.

The approach

Cyst(e)inase depletes the amino acids cysteine and cystine from circulation. Tumor cells lose the building blocks of glutathione and become vulnerable to ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of cell death.

The evidence

A 2026 study in iScience showed that systemic Cyst(e)inase induces ferroptosis in glioblastoma models and works together with temozolomide.
